# AFrame-by-Frame Conditional Diffusion Framework for Controllable Animation Character Video Generation

Controllable character video animation seeks to transfer motion from a reference video onto an animated character, but existing diffusion-based methods struggle to precisely control motion and produce long-duration videos. They often convert text-to-image models into text-to-video frameworks, leading to a trade-off between video length and per-frame quality, and rely heavily on sparse skeletal conditions that can introduce ambiguity. We propose a frame-by-frame conditional generation framework for extended-duration generation with a constant per-frame memory footprint. The first-frame anchor, previous-frame motion condition, and cross-frame attention jointly constrain sequential generation. To address temporal inconsistency inherent in sequential generation, we introduce a coarse-to-fine dual-frame rendering strategy with a motion feature extraction and fusion module that preserves invariant features across frames. In the coarse stage, a multi-conditional inpainting-based renderer enforces inter-frame consistency; in the fine stage, a training-free attention mechanism smooths temporal transitions. Furthermore, by leveraging richer driving conditions beyond sparse skeletons, our framework reduces ambiguity and improves motion fidelity. Our method requires lightweight additional training, remains computationally efficient, and supports high-resolution inference. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations confirm stable extended-duration generation. Extensive experiments show it outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in temporal coherence and per-frame image quality, making it especially accessible for application-oriented communities.
